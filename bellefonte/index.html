If the tank is leaking, turn off the cold supply valve at the top of the tank, then switch off the breaker for an electric model or set the gas control to "off" on a gas model. Move boxes and anything valuable away from the tank and put down towels to slow the spread. Never ignore the smell of gas: get everyone out and call the gas utility from outside.

Keeping Your Bellefonte Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that point and also shows discolored water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts now so help is one call away the moment something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.
