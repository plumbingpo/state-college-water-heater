Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity
  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • Discolored hot water, a sign the tank is corroding inside

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Consider the vent first. Gas water heater exhaust carries carbon monoxide, so an undersized, badly sloped, or loose vent can spill it back into living space. Add a gas connection that has to be leak-free, an electrical circuit that has to match the new heater's load, and a relief valve that has to carry the right rating with a discharge line that is never capped or undersized, and the details pile up quickly. Local code may also call for an expansion tank, drain pan, or seismic straps, and some areas want a permit. Switching to tankless or a heat pump adds new gas line sizing, vent materials, circuits, and condensate lines to the list.

Every gas and water joint is leak-tested, gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check, and the old heater is hauled away. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
